Caveman Odyssey: Sparta

Book IV, Part 1 — a puppet script — after Homer

Puppets Needed

  • TELEMAKOS — far from home now; watches everything
  • PEISISTRATOS — Nestor's son, steady companion
  • MENELAOS — great king, enormous palace, enormous grief; generous without thinking about it
  • HELENE — the most beautiful woman in the world; calm, direct, carries the weight of the war
  • ETEONEOS — servant at gate; small role, played for laughs
Scene 1 — The Gate
ETEONEOSMenelaos. Two strangers at gate. Young men. Look important. Do we take horses? Or send them somewhere else?
MENELAOSEteoneos. You say stupid thing. We have eaten food of many strangers on long road home from Troy. Zeus sends strangers. Take horses. Bring men inside.
Scene 2 — Inside the Palace
Gold everywhere. Silver. Ivory. Telemakos and Peisistratos stare.
TELEMAKOS(whispering) Look at this. This is what inside of Zeus palace must look like. I cannot even speak.
MENELAOSYes. Much gold. I went very far to get it. Eight years wandering before I came home. And while I was gone, someone killed my brother. So I have all this gold — and I would give all of it to have back three men who died. Most of all one man — Odysseus. Somewhere his son grows up without him.
Telemakos pulls cloak over his eyes. Menelaos sees. Says nothing yet.
HELENEMenelaos. Who are these young men? This boy — this is son of Odysseus. This is Telemakos. He was baby when his father went to Troy for my sake. I have never stopped feeling that.
MENELAOSYes. Same hands. Same eyes. I was just speaking of his father.
PEISISTRATOSYes — this is Telemakos, son of Odysseus. I am Peisistratos, son of Nestor. My brother Antilochos died at Troy. I never knew him. But people say he was best fighter there. I carry that.
MENELAOSAntilochos. Yes. I knew him. He was everything they say. You are both welcome here. Eat. Tomorrow we talk about fathers. Tonight — grief can wait one night.
Scene 3 — The Drug
Helene puts something in the wine. Drug from Egypt. Takes away grief. Takes away anger.
HELENELet me tell you about Odysseus. I saw him in Troy — disguised as beggar, wounds, rags, filth. He walked into enemy city to spy. I knew him. I took him inside. He told me Greek plans. I kept his secret. I wanted to go home by then. I was tired of Troy. I was tired of all of it. When he left he killed many Trojans. That was Odysseus — walking into enemy city alone and walking out.
MENELAOSYes. And this — end of war, we were inside wooden horse, all hidden, waiting. You came and walked around outside, calling names of Greek heroes in voices of their wives. Some of us almost answered. Odysseus held us. He put his hand over mouths. He kept us still. One man almost cried out. Odysseus held his mouth shut with both hands until you walked away. That is how we survived. That was Odysseus.
They sit with these stories. Then they sleep.

Puppet Show Tips

  • Eteoneos is comic relief — a small officious man. Menelaos dismissing him ("you say stupid thing") should get a laugh.
  • Telemakos pulling his cloak over his eyes is the emotional hinge of Scene 2. Do not rush past it. Menelaos sees and says nothing — that silence is important.
  • Helene's entrance changes the temperature of the room. She is not performing her beauty — she is direct and carries grief. Play her still.
  • The drug: no need to show it dramatically. Helene simply pours. The audience will feel the effect in how the stories that follow are told — quietly, without flinching.
  • Both war stories (Helene's and Menelaos's) are about Odysseus holding things together under impossible pressure. That is the portrait of the father Telemakos has never known.
