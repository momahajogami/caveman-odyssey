Caveman Odyssey: Old Man of Sea

Book IV, Part 2 — a puppet script — after Homer

Puppets Needed

  • TELEMAKOS — asking the question he has crossed the sea to ask
  • MENELAOS — tells a wild story calmly; a man who has seen everything
  • PROTEUS — offstage voice only; old, grudging, cannot lie
Scene 1 — The Question
TELEMAKOSMenelaos. Now we talk about father. Father alive? Father dead? Tell true thing. Do not make soft story.
MENELAOSYou ask right. I will tell true thing. I learned it from old man who cannot lie — Proteus, Old Man of Sea. I will tell you how I caught him.
Scene 2 — The Catching of Proteus
MENELAOSI was stuck in Egypt. No wind. Ships could not move. Men were starving. Then daughter of Proteus found me on beach. She said: catch my father. He comes out of sea at noon to sleep with his seals. He will try to escape — he changes shape. He becomes lion. He becomes serpent. He becomes water. He becomes tree. Hold on. Do not let go. When he stops — he is tired. Then he must answer.
TELEMAKOSYou held on?
MENELAOSWe held on. Lion. Serpent. Leopard. Boar. Running water. Tall tree. We held on. He stopped. He was angry. He said: ask your questions. I asked about my men — he told me. I asked about Agamemnon — he told me. Then I asked: is there anyone else still alive, still stuck somewhere?
TELEMAKOSWhat did he say.
MENELAOSHe said: son of Laertes. Odysseus. I saw him on island of nymph Kalypso. He was crying. Sitting on shore, looking at sea, crying. He has no ship. He has no crew. He cannot get home. Kalypso keeps him there.
TELEMAKOSAlive. He is alive.
MENELAOSAlive. On island. Crying. Wanting to come home. That is what I know.
Scene 3 — The Departure
MENELAOSStay here. Ten days, twelve days. I will give you gifts — good horses, fine things.
TELEMAKOSMenelaos. Thank you. But I cannot stay. Men at home eat father's animals, drink father's wine. Every day I am gone is one more day they take. Give me gift I can carry on ship. Horses I cannot take to Ithaka — Ithaka is small island, no room to run horses. Keep horses. Give me something else.
MENELAOSGood answer. Right answer. You are son of Odysseus. He would have said same thing. Take this silver bowl. Take this golden jar. Go home. Father is coming.
They embrace. Telemakos takes road back to sea.

Puppet Show Tips

  • Proteus's transformations are the best puppet opportunity in Book IV. Use a single puppet that the puppeteer rapidly swaps accessories on — a mane for lion, a coil for serpent — or simply call out the shapes while the puppet thrashes. The thrashing is the point.
  • "Alive. He is alive." — Telemakos says this very quietly. Everything he has done since Book I was to hear this. Let it land in silence.
  • Menelaos telling the Proteus story should be calm and matter-of-fact. He has seen too much to be dramatic about it. The contrast with the wildness of the story is what makes it funny and real.
  • Telemakos refusing the horses is his most adult moment so far — practical, clear-eyed, thinking about home. Play it without sentiment. He has grown up.
  • "Father is coming." — the last line. The last word of the Telemakheia. Say it simply.
