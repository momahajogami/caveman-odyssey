The Stranger at the Door

Book I, Part 2 — Athena comes to Ithaca. A boy starts to become a man.

Ithaca. The big house of Odysseus. Suitors everywhere — eating, drinking, very loud, throwing things. Telemakos sits alone in the corner. Sad. Thinking about his father. A stranger appears at the door.
Telemakos sees the stranger. Jumps up. Runs over. The suitors do not get up.
Telemakos

Stranger! Welcome! Come in! Eat! Rest! We talk after.

He brings the stranger inside, away from the suitors. They sit together. Food comes.
Athena disguised as Mentes, old sea captain

Thank you. I am Mentes. Old friend of your father. I come to visit Odysseus. But — I see house full of strangers. Loud strangers. Eating everything. What happen here?

Telemakos quietly, ashamed

Father not come home from Troy. Long time now. Maybe dead. Nobody know. These men — they want marry mother. Take father house. Take everything. They eat all food. Every day. They stay and stay. They not go.

Mother not pick husband. She wait for father. But she cannot wait forever.

Athena

Your father is not dead. I know this. Odysseus is stuck — held somewhere far away. But he is coming home. Maybe slow. But coming.

Telemakos

How you know this?

Athena

I know. Listen to me now. You need to do two things.

One: tomorrow, call assembly. Tell these men out loud, in front of everyone: go home. Say it with your chest. Make them hear it.

Two: get a boat. Go find news of your father. Go ask old king Nestor at Pylos. Go ask king Menelaos at Sparta. They were at Troy. They know things. Maybe your father alive. Maybe dead. Either way — you need to know. A man needs to know.

Telemakos

You sound like... you really believe father coming back.

Athena

I do. Now — look at these men eating your food. Look at them. Your father built this house. Your father earned all of this. Do not let them have it.

* * *
Across the hall, Phemios the bard is singing. His song is about the Greek heroes coming home from Troy. It is a sad song. Many did not make it. Penelope hears the song from upstairs. She comes down, crying.
Penelope Telemakos's mother

Phemios. Please. Not that song. Too many sad songs about Troy. Too sad. Sing something else.

The room goes quiet. Everyone looks. Then —
Telemakos standing up, surprised at himself

Mother. Go upstairs. This is men's business. Phemios sings what he wants. You go rest. I will handle things here.

Penelope stares at her son. Something is different. She goes upstairs without arguing. The suitors stare too.
Antinoos to the other suitors, laughing but a little uneasy

Ha. Little boy talking big. Very funny.

But the laughter is not quite as easy as before. They go back to eating. Evening comes. Suitors leave for the night. The stranger says goodbye and goes.
Telemakos stands alone in the quiet hall for a moment. Then he goes to bed. For the first time in a long time, he is not just sad. He has a plan.
Caveman Odyssey — after Homer — Ralph Hutchison
