While Telemakhos is in the storeroom with Eurykleia, Mentor is moving through Ithaka.

He goes from house to house through the evening — the houses of young men, sons of good families, men who knew Odysseus or whose fathers did. He knocks, speaks quietly, gives a time and a place. He does not explain more than is necessary. Come to the harbor tonight. Bring nothing — everything is provided.

They come. One by one, then in pairs, they make their way down to the water in the dark. Twenty of them — young, able, curious in the way young men are curious about anything that might be a story later.

Mentor is already there when the first of them arrive. The ship is Noemon's — borrowed without a full explanation, the kind of loan that gets made when the asker has a certain quality of certainty about them. It is a good ship, fast, well-fitted.

They load the provisions as they come down from the palace — the sealed jars of wine, the skins of barley meal, the ship's gear. They do it without much talk. The harbor is quiet. The town above them is dark. The suitors' fires show in a few windows of the palace, still burning.

The ship sits in the water and waits. The men sit on the rowing benches and wait.

The night is still.
