When he gets back to the palace, the suitors are already eating again.

Antinoos sees him come in and calls across the hall — a friendly voice, which is worse than a hostile one. "Telemakhos! Come eat with us. Stop all this business about ships and sailing. Have some wine."

Telemakhos doesn't answer. He walks through the hall and the suitors watch him go, and some of them laugh. He doesn't look back.

He finds Eurykleia in the inner rooms and takes her to the storeroom — the long stone room at the back of the house where his father's wine and oil and grain were kept. It smells of beeswax and old wood and something he cannot name, some quality of accumulation, years of careful keeping.

"I need twelve jars of wine," he says. "Sealed. And twenty measures of barley meal in good skins. Prepare them and set them aside. I'll tell you when I need them moved."

Eurykleia looks at him. She has known him since the day he was born. She has known this house since before he was born. She does not ask yet.

"And tell no one," he says.

He begins to check the jars himself — lifting each one, checking the seal. He works quickly and quietly while Eurykleia watches him from the doorway.
