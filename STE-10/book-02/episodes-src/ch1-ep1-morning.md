Dawn comes pale and early over Ithaka.

Telemakhos wakes. He lies still for a moment, then gets up. He ties on his sandals — the fine ones, with the bronze clasps. He picks up his bronze-tipped spear and walks out of his room into the courtyard.

The servants are already moving. They see him cross the yard and they look at each other. He walks the way his father used to walk — the same set of the shoulders, the same unhurried pace. It is a strange thing to watch, a boy who has spent sixteen years becoming slowly visible as someone else.

He calls the herald and asks him to summon the people of Ithaka to the meeting-place. The herald goes. Telemakhos walks down through the town to the assembly ground.

He is the first to arrive. He sits in his father's seat — the carved stone chair at the center, the one that has been empty for ten years — and waits while the elders come filing in around him.
