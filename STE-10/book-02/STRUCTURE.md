# Book II — Episode Structure
## Very Small Homer / STE-10

Book II is Telemakhos executing the plan. He calls the assembly, stands up in
front of all of Ithaka, and asks the suitors to leave. They refuse. An eagle
omen comes and is dismissed. The assembly collapses with nothing resolved.
He is given a ship by Athena-disguised. He loads it in secret. He leaves in the
middle of the night without telling his mother.

Every episode moves him further from the boy who sat in the corner.

---

## CHAPTER 1 — The Assembly

*Morning. Ithaka comes to the meeting-place for the first time in twenty years.
Telemakhos has called it. He speaks. The suitors answer. An omen arrives and
is dismissed. The assembly ends with nothing changed — except Telemakhos.*

### Episode 1.1 — "Morning"
**Homer lines:** 1–14
**What happens:** Telemakhos wakes at dawn. He ties on his sandals, picks up
his bronze spear, walks out. The servants see him and think of his father.
He is the first to arrive at the assembly ground.
**Dramatic purpose:** Open Book II with the same unhurried precision as Homer.
The act of dressing, arming, walking — it is not nothing. It is the first
deliberate movement of his life.
**Ending image:** He sits in his father's seat. The elders come and fill in
around him.

---

### Episode 1.2 — "Telemakhos Speaks"
**Homer lines:** 15–79
**What happens:** Telemakhos takes the scepter and speaks. He tells everyone
what the suitors are doing. He asks the people of Ithaka to do something —
or at least to feel shame. He weeps mid-speech. He throws the scepter to the
ground when he sits.
**Dramatic purpose:** The speech itself is remarkable — not polished, not
strategic. He is angry and grieving in public for the first time. The weeping
and the thrown scepter are not weakness; they are the only honest response to
the situation.
**Ending image:** The scepter on the ground. The hall silent.

---

### Episode 1.3 — "Antinoos Answers"
**Homer lines:** 80–145
**What happens:** Antinoos stands and blames Penelope. He tells the story of
the shroud — three years of weaving and unweaving, buying time, until a servant
betrayed her. Eurymakhos follows, saying the same thing more smoothly. Neither
of them addresses Telemakhos's actual request.
**Dramatic purpose:** The suitors are not stupid — they cannot afford to be.
By turning the argument to Penelope's deception, they reframe themselves as the
wronged parties. It is ruthless and it almost works.
**Ending image:** Telemakhos stands again. "I will not force my mother from her
house. She bore me. Whatever else — I will not do that."

---

### Episode 1.4 — "The Eagles"
**Homer lines:** 146–207
**What happens:** Two eagles appear, fighting in midair above the assembly, then
separate. An old man — Halitherses, who has read omens for fifty years — stands
up and interprets: Odysseus is close to home, and doom is coming for the suitors.
Eurymakhos stands up and mocks him. Threatens him. Sit down, old man. Your bird
means nothing.
**Dramatic purpose:** The gods have just sent a sign. The suitors choose not to
see it. This is the moment the audience understands what is coming and the suitors
do not.
**Ending image:** The eagles are gone. The sky is empty. Halitherses sits down.

---

### Episode 1.5 — "The Assembly Ends"
**Homer lines:** 208–256
**What happens:** Mentor — Odysseus's old friend, left in charge of the household
— stands and speaks. He does not address the suitors; he addresses the people of
Ithaka. He says: you are all complicit. You sit and watch and do nothing. He is
shouted down. Leokritos, another suitor, dismisses the assembly. Everyone goes
home. Telemakhos walks down to the shore alone.
**Dramatic purpose:** The assembly was Telemakhos's first act, and it failed
completely in practical terms. But something has changed: he has spoken, been
heard, been refused. He knows exactly what the situation is now.
**Ending image:** The shore. The water. Telemakhos alone, washing his hands.

## CHAPTER 2 — The Ship

*The assembly gave him nothing. Now he has to find a ship, a crew, and
provisions — without the suitors finding out. Athena helps. Eurykleia helps.
Neither of them wants him to go.*

### Episode 2.1 — "Athena on the Shore"
**Homer lines:** 257–336
**What happens:** Athena appears in the form of Mentor — the real Mentor, the
old friend. She finds Telemakhos at the water's edge. She tells him: stop
grieving, start moving. Your father had courage. Show some. I will get you a
ship and crew. Go home, prepare provisions, and meet me at the harbor tonight.
**Dramatic purpose:** The second Athena-as-guide scene. The tone is sharper than
in Book I — less gentle, more impatient. Telemakhos has had the plan for a day
now. It is time to execute it.
**Ending image:** Mentor is gone. Telemakhos turns from the water and walks back
toward the palace. His step is different.

---

### Episode 2.2 — "The Storeroom"
**Homer lines:** 337–381
**What happens:** He gets home. The suitors are at it again — eating, laughing,
at ease. Antinoos calls out to him, mockingly inviting him to eat and stay.
Telemakhos ignores him. He finds Eurykleia — his old nurse — and takes her to the
storeroom. He asks her to fill twelve jars with wine, twenty measures of barley
meal. Secretly. Tell no one. He is leaving tonight.
**Dramatic purpose:** The contrast between the suitors' noise and Telemakhos
moving quietly through his own house, loading provisions in secret. He has
learned something from a night's thought.
**Ending image:** The jars lined up. Eurykleia looks at him. She already knows
something is wrong.

---

### Episode 2.3 — "The Oath"
**Homer lines:** 382–434
**What happens:** Eurykleia grabs his hands. She asks why. She says: you are all
your mother has. Your father is already gone. Don't make her lose you too.
Telemakhos says: the gods sent me. I have to go. He tells her not to tell Penelope
for twelve days, or until Penelope asks where he is. Then he gives her his hand
and makes her swear.
**Dramatic purpose:** Eurykleia's grief is not performance — she raised him, she
raised his father, she has outlived more than most people see. Her fear is real.
His resolve is also real. Neither is wrong.
**Ending image:** She swears. She goes to fill the jars. He stands alone in the
storeroom for a moment.

---

## CHAPTER 3 — The Night

*After midnight. Athena casts sleep on the suitors. Telemakhos slips out of
his own house like a thief. The ship is ready. The wind is ready.*

### Episode 3.1 — "The Crew"
**Homer lines:** 382–434 (parallel action), 386–434
**What happens:** While Telemakhos is with Eurykleia, Athena is moving through
the town in Mentor's shape, finding men. She goes to each of the twenty she has
chosen — young men, sons of good families, friends of Telemakhos — and calls
them to the harbor at dark. They come. They load the ship with the provisions
Telemakhos sent down.
**Dramatic purpose:** A brief scene outside the palace — the world of the
harbor, the quiet loading, the ordinary young men who don't know what they are
sailing into.
**Ending image:** The ship loaded and waiting. The harbor still. The town
dark above it.

---

### Episode 3.2 — "The Dark Departure"
**Homer lines:** 435–end
**What happens:** Athena goes through the palace and pours sleep on the suitors
— each one sinks where he sits. Telemakhos takes Mentor's hand at the shore.
They board. The crew rows out of the harbor. Then Athena calls a wind — sharp,
from the west — and the sail fills and they move. The island of Ithaka falls
behind them. Telemakhos does not look back.
**Dramatic purpose:** End Book II the way it should end: with motion, with
darkness, with wind. The first real thing Telemakhos has ever done.
**Ending image:** He does not look back.
