Chapter 1 · Episode 1.4

The Eagles

Book II, lines 146–207 — An omen arrives. Halitherses reads it. Eurymakhos refuses it.

While they are sitting there, two eagles appear.

They come from the mountains, high up, riding the same current, flying side by side. They circle over the assembly once. Then they turn on each other — wings beating, talons out, tearing at each other's heads and necks — and then they break apart and fly off in different directions and are gone.

The assembly goes quiet. Everyone saw it.

An old man named Halitherses stands up. He has been reading omens for fifty years — birds, entrails, lightning, the behavior of animals. He knows what he saw.

"Listen to me," he says. "Those eagles are a sign. Odysseus is not far from home. He is coming. And when he comes, there will be grief for every man in that hall — I am telling you now, while there is still time. I have been right before. I was right about Odysseus when he sailed for Troy — I said he would suffer much and come home alone after twenty years. I was right then. I am right now."

He sits down.

Eurymakhos stands immediately. He is not interested in the old man's omens and he does not bother to hide it. "Halitherses," he says, "go home and prophesy to your own children. Birds fly east and birds fly west and none of it means anything. Odysseus is dead. He died far away and there is nothing to read in two eagles fighting. And if you keep telling the boy here that his father is coming back, you are doing him harm." He turns to Telemakhos. "We are not leaving. Your mother needs to choose. That is the end of it."

The sky above the assembly is empty. The eagles are gone.
