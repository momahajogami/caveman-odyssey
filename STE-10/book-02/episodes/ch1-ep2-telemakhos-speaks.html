Chapter 1 · Episode 1.2

Telemakhos Speaks

Book II, lines 15–79 — His first public speech. He weeps. He throws the scepter down.

When the assembly is full, Telemakhos stands. The herald puts the speaking-scepter in his hands.

He looks out at the people of Ithaka. He has never spoken in an assembly before. He begins.

He tells them about the suitors. He says his mother will not choose among them and cannot send them away. He says they are eating through his father's estate — the cattle, the wine, the stores that took years to build. He asks the men of Ithaka: do something. Or at least feel ashamed. Look at what is happening in Odysseus's house and feel ashamed.

His voice breaks. He is trying to hold it and he cannot. Tears come. He is sixteen and he is asking a room full of men to act, and he already knows they will not.

He throws the scepter to the ground and sits down.

An old man beside him picks it up quietly and gives it back. The hall is silent for a moment. Then the silence fills with murmuring — people shifting, looking at each other, not quite meeting the boy's eyes.

Then Antinoos stands.
