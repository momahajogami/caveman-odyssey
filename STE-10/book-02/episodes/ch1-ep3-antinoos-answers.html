Chapter 1 · Episode 1.3

Antinoos Answers

Book II, lines 80–145 — The suitors blame Penelope. Telemakhos will not give her up.

Antinoos is not embarrassed. He is never embarrassed.

"Telemakhos," he says, "this high talk — blaming us. You should be blaming your mother." He looks out at the assembly, playing to the crowd. "You want to know why we are still in that house? Let me tell you what your mother did."

He tells the story of the shroud. Penelope told the suitors she would choose when she had finished weaving a burial shroud for Laertes — Odysseus's aged father, who would die someday and needed one. They agreed to wait. She wove every day, and every night she unraveled it. For three years. They only found out because one of her own servants came and told them.

"So the shroud is finished now," Antinoos says. "And she still will not choose. The gods gave her a clever mind — we grant her that. But the cost of her cleverness is ours. Tell her to choose. Whichever of us she wants, let her go to his house. Then we will leave yours."

Eurymakhos follows with the same speech, more smoothly worded. The message is identical: Penelope is the problem. Telemakhos should fix his mother.

Telemakhos stands again. His voice is steady now. "I will not force my mother out of her own house," he says. "She bore me. She raised me. Whatever else is true — I will not do that." He pauses. "But I am asking the gods, and I am asking the men of Ithaka: look at this situation and judge it."

He sits down. Antinoos shrugs and sits down too.
