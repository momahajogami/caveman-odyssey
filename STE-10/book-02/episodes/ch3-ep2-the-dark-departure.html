Chapter 3 · Episode 3.2

The Dark Departure

Book II, lines 435–end — Sleep on the suitors. Telemakhos to the ship. The wind comes.

Athena moves through the palace.

She goes from room to room, and the suitors' wine-heavy eyes grow heavier. One by one they lay their heads down — on the tables, on their arms, on the floor where they sat. They are all asleep before any of them noticed getting sleepy. The fires burn low. The hall fills with the sound of breathing.

Telemakhos steps over them.

He takes his cloak and walks out through the gate, down the path toward the harbor. The night air is cold and smells of the sea. His sandals are quiet on the stone.

Mentor is waiting at the water's edge. The ship is there, the crew in their places, the oars shipped, the sail furled. Everything ready.

Telemakhos steps aboard. He sits at the stern beside Mentor. The crew runs out the oars and rows them out of the harbor — quiet, clean strokes, the water folding back on either side in the dark.

When they clear the headland, Mentor lifts his hand and a wind comes — sharp, from the west, exactly what they need. The sail is set and fills at once. The ship leans into it and begins to move with purpose. The oars come in.

Ithaka is behind them now — the lights of the palace small and yellow in the dark.

Telemakhos does not look back.
