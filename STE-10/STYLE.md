# Very Small Homer — STE-10 Style Guide

*For authors and collaborators working in the STE-10 voice.*

---

## What STE-10 Is

A prose-narrative retelling of Homer for readers who are ready for the real story
but not yet for full Homeric verse. Clear sentences, full grammar, present tense.
Every word earns its place. The stakes are real and the feelings are real.

Where the Caveman edition strips to pure dialogue, STE-10 adds a narrator — lean,
observant, never intrusive — who can put the reader in the room.

---

## The Voice

### Core rules

- **Present tense throughout.** Not "Telemakhos walked to the gate" — "Telemakhos
  walks to the gate." Present tense keeps the story immediate.
- **Short paragraphs.** Never more than four sentences. If a paragraph wants to be
  five sentences, it wants to be two paragraphs.
- **The narrator can feel things.** Not "Eurykleia walked in" but "Eurykleia moves
  around the room the way she has moved around rooms her whole life — without wasted
  steps, doing the next thing that needs doing." One quiet observation per scene,
  placed where it lands hardest.
- **Characters speak in quoted dialogue.** Embed it in narrative, not in labeled
  blocks. Let the prose carry it in and out.
- **No subordinate clauses stacked on subordinate clauses.** Two levels of nesting,
  maximum. Break it apart.
- **Vocabulary:** Any word a confident ten-year-old can absorb from context is fine.
  Do not define words in the text. Trust the reader.

### What the narrator does not do

Does not explain emotions. Does not tell the reader how to feel. Does not editorialize.
The narrator shows the torches, the empty road, the folded tunic. The reader feels
what that means.

Does not recap. Every episode assumes the reader has read the previous one.

Does not soften the story. The grief is real. The danger is real. The humor is real.

### Proper names

Follow the Greek spellings used throughout the project:

- Telemakhos (not Telemachus)
- Ithaka (not Ithaca)
- Kalypso (not Calypso)
- Aithiopians (not Ethiopians)
- Athena, Odysseus, Zeus, Poseidon (same in both traditions)
- Aigisthos (not Aegisthus)
- Eurykleia (not Eurycleia)

## Episode Structure

### Length

Each episode should take 4–7 minutes to read aloud. Shorter is fine. Longer means
split it.

### The ending image

End on something concrete — an action, a detail, a sentence of stillness. Not on
dialogue if you can avoid it. Not on summary.

Examples from Book I:
- "The Muse begins." (1.1)
- "The fires go out one by one." (4.2)
- "There is no one on it in either direction." (4.1)

### Chapters as dramatic phases

Each chapter covers one phase of a scene: one location, one unit of time, one
emotional arc. Episodes within a chapter are close-ups on that phase — individual
moments that can breathe on their own.

The chapter title names the phase. The episode titles name the moments.

---

## Two-Level Navigation

Each episode page shows its chapter number, episode number, and navigates to
previous/next episode. The book index shows all chapters and episodes at a glance.

File naming: `ch{N}-ep{N}-{slug}.html`

Example: `ch2-ep1-mentes-at-the-gate.html`

## What We're Building

STE-10 covers the whole Odyssey at this level of detail: many episodes per book,
organized into chapters. The prose voice is consistent throughout. The story is
Homer's — condensed, clarified, never simplified into something smaller than it is.
