Chapter 2 · Episode 2.3

The Bard Sings

Book I, lines 319–364 — Penelope comes downstairs. Telemakhos finds his voice.

Phemios is singing about the Greeks sailing home from Troy.

It is a famous song — Phemios is the best bard on the island, and he sings it well. He sings about the different captains, the different routes, who was wrecked and who was spared. He sings about Agamemnon sailing home to his own murder. He sings about the ones who arrived safely to their fires and wives. It is a song about endings, about what it means to finally be home.

He does not sing about Odysseus. There is no ending to that story yet.

Penelope hears the song from the upper floor of the palace. She comes down the stairs slowly, a shawl over her head, two servants close behind her. She is still beautiful. She is also worn out in a way that has nothing to do with lack of sleep.

"Phemios," she says. The hall goes quiet enough that everyone hears. "You know a thousand songs. You know how many songs there are in the world. Why this one? Stop. Sing something else. Any other song."

Phemios looks uncertain. The suitors watch.

Telemakhos stands up. He has just spent an hour talking to Mentes. Something has shifted in him, even if he cannot name it yet. He speaks clearly, in a voice that carries.

"Mother. The bard can sing what he likes. Poets give the listeners what they want, and that's not Phemios's fault or yours. And Odysseus is not the only man who didn't come home from Troy." He pauses. "Go upstairs. Take care of your loom, your weaving, your work. What happens in this hall is mine to manage now."

The suitors are very still. This is not the Telemakhos they are used to.

Penelope stares at her son. She sees something in his face she has not seen before. She turns and goes back upstairs without another word. The servants follow.

Upstairs, alone, she cries. She does not know yet what has changed. But something has.

Downstairs, Phemios plays on. The suitors go back to eating. Mentes says nothing. Telemakhos sits back down beside him and picks up his cup and they both stare at the hall for a moment in a new kind of quiet.
