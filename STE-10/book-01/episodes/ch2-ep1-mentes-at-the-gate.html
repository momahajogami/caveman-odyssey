Chapter 2 · Episode 2.1

Mentes at the Gate

Book I, lines 96–155 — Athena arrives in Ithaka. Telemakhos is the first to see her.

The hall of Odysseus in Ithaka is full of men who should not be there.

They have killed oxen from Odysseus's herds and spread the skins across the floor to sit on. They eat bread from Odysseus's stores, drink wine from his jars, and call for more. Servants move back and forth, eyes down, arms full. In the corner, Phemios the bard plays his lyre for men who treat music as background noise. The hall smells of roasting meat and spilled wine and too many people who have been somewhere too long.

At the gates, standing alone, a stranger waits.

Telemakhos is the first to see him. He is sixteen, and he spends a great deal of time sitting apart from the noise, thinking about his father. He notices the stranger — a man, to all appearances, lean and sun-dark, carrying a bronze spear, with the look of someone who has come a long way — and he gets up without being asked.

"Stranger," he says, "you're welcome here. Come inside, eat. Tell me who you are when you've had enough."

He takes the bronze spear and sets it in the spear-rack. He leads the stranger to a good chair, set apart from the suitors, and brings food himself — bread, meat, a cup of wine. The suitors eat and drink and do not look up. They rarely notice what Telemakhos does.

The stranger's name, when Telemakhos finally asks, is Mentes — a sea-captain from the Taphians, he says, carrying iron to trade. He watches the hall with quiet attention while Telemakhos pours more wine.

"What is all this?" Mentes asks. His voice is even, but his eyes have taken in everything: the number of men, the state of the floor, the servants' exhaustion.

Telemakhos looks at the suitors for a moment. His face does not change. "It's a long story," he says.
