Chapter 3 · Episode 3.3

Antinoos and Eurymakhos

Book I, lines 365–420 — The suitors notice that Telemakhos is different tonight.

The evening goes long. More food comes. More wine. Phemios changes to something louder.

Antinoos has been watching Telemakhos all night. He is one of the leaders of the suitors — a big man, confident, used to having his way in any room. He has watched Telemakhos sit in the corner with the stranger all evening, heads together, and he does not like what he is seeing.

He comes over when Mentes steps away. "Look at you," he says to Telemakhos. His voice is friendly in the way that a large dog can seem friendly. "All evening with your sea-captain friend, whispering. You're becoming very interesting lately. Did Zeus send someone to teach you things you didn't know?"

Telemakhos looks at him steadily. "My father had an old friend in the islands. He came to pay his respects and brought news. I gave him hospitality." He pauses. "That's what this hall is supposed to be for."

Antinoos laughs and goes back to his table.

Then Eurymakhos comes over. He is the second of the suitor leaders, and where Antinoos is blunt, Eurymakhos is smooth — handsome, careful with every word, always measuring. He sits down as if he is just being friendly. "Who was that stranger?" he asks. "Where is he from? What did he want with you?" His tone is mild. His eyes are not.

"My father's old friend," Telemakhos says. "He brought news of my father." A beat. "I am calling an assembly tomorrow. I think you should be there."

Eurymakhos holds his gaze for a moment. He nods once, as if filing the information away, and walks back to his table without another word.

The suitors go back to their games. Antinoos says something to Eurymakhos too quietly to hear. Telemakhos does not try to hear it. He has said what he needed to say. The assembly comes next. Tomorrow is a different kind of day.
