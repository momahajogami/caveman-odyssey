# Book I — Episode Structure
## Very Small Homer / STE-10

Book I in Homer runs from the invocation of the Muse through Telemakhos falling
asleep the night his life changes. Everything that drives the next three books
is set in motion here: the gods decide Odysseus comes home; Athena lights a
fire under Telemakhos; the suitors show their full ugliness; Penelope's grief
becomes visible; Telemakhos gets a plan.

The two-level structure below: **Chapters** (dramatic phases) containing
**Episodes** (scenes that can stand alone and be read in one sitting).

---

## The Voice — STE-10

Clear, present-tense prose. Short paragraphs — never more than four sentences.
No caveman compression; full grammar; but no fat either. Every sentence either
moves the story or reveals character.

Third-person narrator can describe what the characters see and feel. Characters
speak in quoted dialogue. The narrator is allowed exactly one quiet observation
per scene — a detail that lands without comment.

Vocabulary: any word a confident ten-year-old can absorb from context is fine.
Never talk down. The stakes are real. The grief is real. The humor is real.

## CHAPTER 1 — The Singer Calls

*Before the story starts, the singer asks the Muse for help. Then we learn
what the gods have decided. These two episodes are the frame that holds
everything else.*

### Episode 1.1 — "The Muse"
**Homer lines:** 1–21
**What happens:** A singer steps forward. He asks a goddess — the Muse — to
help him tell the story of a man who is lost. We learn: the man is clever,
brave, and has been trying to get home for ten years. His crew is dead. He
is alone on an island. His name is Odysseus.
**Dramatic purpose:** Orient the audience. Establish the emotional stakes —
longing for home — before a single scene plays out.
**Ending image:** "The singer stops. The Muse knows this man. She begins."

---

### Episode 1.2 — "The Gods Decide"
**Homer lines:** 22–95
**What happens:** The gods are feasting on Olympos. Poseidon is away. Athena
speaks to Zeus — she is angry that Odysseus suffers while the gods do nothing.
Zeus agrees: it is time. Hermes will go to the island of Kalypso. Athena will
go to Ithaka and find Odysseus's son. They will set things moving.
**Dramatic purpose:** Establish the divine machinery. Show Athena's fierce
loyalty. Give the audience the god's-eye view before we descend to earth.
**Ending image:** Athena pulls a disguise around her like a coat and steps off
the edge of Olympos.
**Key character beat:** Athena does not ask politely. She accuses. Zeus bends
not because she pleads — but because she is right.

---

## CHAPTER 2 — The Hall of the Suitors

*Ithaka. The palace of Odysseus — except it is not Odysseus's palace anymore.
A hundred men are eating his food, drinking his wine, and competing to marry
his wife. His son watches them from the corner.*

### Episode 2.1 — "Mentes at the Gate"
**Homer lines:** 96–155
**What happens:** Athena arrives at the gates of Ithaka disguised as Mentes,
a sea-captain Odysseus knew. Telemakhos sees her standing alone while the
suitors ignore her. He brings her in, seats her, offers food, asks her name.
**Dramatic purpose:** First sight of Ithaka. First sight of Telemakhos. The
contrast between his courtesy and the suitors' noise establishes who he is.
**Ending image:** Phemios the bard starts singing. Telemakhos leans toward
Mentes and lowers his voice.

---

### Episode 2.2 — "What the Boy Carries"
**Homer lines:** 156–251
**What happens:** Telemakhos tells Mentes everything. The suitors arrived
after his father disappeared. They eat, drink, play games, refuse to leave.
His mother will not choose one of them and will not send them away. Telemakhos
doesn't know if his father is alive or dead. He has grown up not knowing.
**Dramatic purpose:** The full weight of Telemakhos's situation. He is not
passive from laziness — he is passive because there is nothing a young man
alone can do against a hundred armed men.
**Ending image:** He shrugs. "I don't even know if I am really his son. No one
can know that."
**Key character beat:** This last line is not self-pity. It is a hard fact
about the world he has grown up in.

---

### Episode 2.3 — "The Bard Sings"
**Homer lines:** 319–364
**What happens:** Phemios is singing a song about the Greeks sailing home
from Troy — some making it, some not. Penelope comes downstairs and hears it.
She asks him to stop. The song is too close to her own grief. Telemakhos
speaks up: Phemios can sing what he likes. Penelope should go back upstairs.
**Dramatic purpose:** First sight of Penelope. First time Telemakhos asserts
himself publicly. It is a small act — silencing his mother at a feast — but
it is the first crack in ten years of silence.
**Ending image:** Penelope goes back upstairs. She cries alone. She does not
know it yet, but her son is changing.
**Note:** Telemakhos is not being cruel. He is practicing. He has just heard
from Mentes what he is supposed to do next.

---

## CHAPTER 3 — The Stranger's Words

*Mentes gives Telemakhos his instructions. The suitors try to intimidate him
at the end of the evening. He does not back down.*

### Episode 3.1 — "What Mentes Knows"
**Homer lines:** 252–306
**What happens:** Mentes tells Telemakhos what he knows of Odysseus. He was
alive when last heard of — held on an island by a goddess who wants to keep
him. He has no ship. But Mentes is sure: he will come home. He is the kind
of man who comes home.
**Dramatic purpose:** The first outside perspective on Odysseus. For Telemakhos,
hearing a stranger speak confidently of his father's return is almost more than
he can hold.
**Ending image:** Mentes looks at Telemakhos a long time. "You have his eyes,"
he says. "I thought so at the gate."

---

### Episode 3.2 — "The Instructions"
**Homer lines:** 252–318 (continued)
**What happens:** Mentes lays out the plan. Call an assembly of Ithaka —
tell the suitors to leave. Then get a ship. Sail to Pylos, find old King
Nestor. Sail to Sparta, find King Menelaos. Ask them: have they heard anything
about Odysseus? If your father is dead — come home and decide what to do. If
he is alive — wait one more year and trust him.
**Dramatic purpose:** The plot of Books II–IV is all here. Telemakhos gets
something he has never had: a plan.
**Ending image:** Telemakhos is quiet for a moment. Then: "I will do it."

---

### Episode 3.3 — "Antinoos and Eurymakhos"
**Homer lines:** 365–420
**What happens:** Evening. The suitors notice that Telemakhos has been sitting
with the stranger all night. Antinoos — the most aggressive — comes over and
mocks him. Eurymakhos — the smoothest — asks questions designed to unsettle.
Telemakhos answers both of them differently than he has ever answered before.
He does not apologize. He does not look down. He tells them the stranger was
his father's old friend. He tells them the assembly will be called tomorrow.
**Dramatic purpose:** The suitors feel the change in Telemakhos before they
understand it. Antinoos is contemptuous. Eurymakhos is watching more carefully.
**Ending image:** The suitors go back to their games. Antinoos says something
under his breath. Telemakhos doesn't answer.

---

## CHAPTER 4 — After Dark

*Mentes leaves. Telemakhos knows what he knows. The night holds still.*

### Episode 4.1 — "The Stranger Gone"
**Homer lines:** 421–444
**What happens:** Mentes says it is time to go. He leaves quickly — too
quickly, almost, for a man who came by sea. Telemakhos watches him go and
realizes something. A thought forms that he does not quite let himself think
all the way. He goes back inside.
**Dramatic purpose:** The moment of recognition — Telemakhos suspects this
was not a mortal man. He does not need to say it aloud. Homer doesn't, either.
**Ending image:** The gate is empty. The torches are burning. Telemakhos
stands there a moment, then turns.
**Note:** Resist explaining. The reader and Telemakhos realize together.

---

### Episode 4.2 — "Eurykleia"
**Homer lines:** 428–444
**What happens:** Eurykleia — the old nurse who raised Telemakhos from birth —
carries a torch and takes him to his room. She has served this family since
before he was born. She folds his clothes. She locks the door. While she
does, she remembers Odysseus as a young man.
**Dramatic purpose:** Close the episode with a quiet human image. The divine
machinery, the suitors' noise, the plan — all of it falls away. An old woman
folds a boy's shirt in the dark.
**Ending image:** Telemakhos lies down. He does not sleep for a long time.
Outside: the suitors' fires are going out, one by one.

## What Comes Next

After Book I, Telemakhos has a plan. Book II is the execution: calling the
assembly, arguing with the suitors publicly, and slipping away in the night
against his nurse's wishes. Each of those moments will need its own chapter.
The structural principle holds across all books: find the chapters (dramatic
phases) first, then the episodes (scenes that breathe on their own).
