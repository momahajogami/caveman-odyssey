Mentes says it is time to go.

He thanks Telemakhos for the seat and the food. He says a few more things about the journey ahead — about what Nestor is like, how old kings who have fought in great wars can sometimes be hard to redirect once they start talking, and how to be patient with that. Then he says goodbye, picks up his bronze spear from the rack, and goes to the door.

He is at the door. Then he is through the door. Then he is gone.

Telemakhos walks to the gate. He stands there in the torchlight and looks out at the road. A man on foot, walking away from the palace, should still be visible — the road runs straight for a long stretch before it bends toward the lower town. The road is empty. There is no one on it in either direction. Only the torches burning, and the sound of the sea somewhere below.

He stands there for a moment. A thought begins to form that he does not let himself finish. He is sixteen years old and he has just been told, by someone he has never met before today, that his father is alive. He has just been given a plan. He has just, for the first time in his life, silenced a room full of adults.

He lets the thought get as far as it wants to go, and then he turns and walks back into the noise of the hall.
