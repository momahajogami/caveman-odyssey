The singer stands at the beginning of the story and cannot see the end of it. He knows only this: there is a man, and the man is trying to get home.

He calls on the Muse — the goddess who remembers all songs, all stories, all the things that happened before anyone was born to witness them. He says: tell me about the man. Tell me about the man of many ways, who traveled far after the war, who saw the cities of many peoples and learned how they lived. He suffered on the sea. He tried to bring his crew home and could not. They died because of their own foolishness, eating the cattle of the sun god Helios when they had been told not to. He could not save them.

The man has been gone from home for ten years. He is not dead. He is on an island far from Ithaka, and he cannot leave it. Every morning he sits on the shore and looks out at the sea and wants to go home more than he has ever wanted anything. His name is Odysseus.

Tell me his story from wherever you know it, the singer says. Begin where you like.

The Muse begins.
