High on Olympos, the gods are feasting.

Poseidon is not there. He has gone to the edge of the world to visit the Aithiopians, who honor him above all other gods. While he is away, the other gods sit with their wine and their long thoughts, and Athena cannot stay quiet any longer.

She is the goddess of wisdom, gray-eyed, fierce, and she has been watching Odysseus for years. She turns to Zeus, king of the gods, and she does not ask politely. She accuses.

"Odysseus has done nothing wrong," she says. "He has prayed to you. He has made offerings. He has honored the gods his whole life. And he sits on an island weeping, held by a goddess who will not let him leave, and the rest of us do nothing. How can you let this go on? Does it mean nothing to you?"

Zeus listens. He says: "I have not forgotten Odysseus. He is the best of mortal men. But Poseidon has not forgotten either. Odysseus blinded Poseidon's son — the Cyclops, Polyphemos — and Poseidon makes every sea hostile to him. We cannot make Poseidon forgive. But we can act while he is away from the table."

Athena straightens. "Then send Hermes to the island of Kalypso. Tell her: the gods have decided, let him go. I will go to Ithaka myself. Odysseus has a son there — Telemakhos — growing up without a father in a house full of men who should not be there. I want to wake him up. I want to give him something to do."

Zeus nods. "Go. Do what you intend. Odysseus will come home."

Athena pulls a disguise around her shoulders like a coat. She steps to the edge of Olympos and drops into the world below, and she does not look back.
