That night they sacrifice to Athena — a heifer with gilded horns, killed at the altar by the palace gate.

Nestor himself pours the libation. He is a man who has made ten thousand offerings in his life, and he makes this one carefully, with the particular attention he gives to things that matter. He thanks her for the sign she gave them. He asks her to bless his house and the son of Odysseus who is sleeping under his roof.

The smoke goes up straight in the still air. Telemakhos watches it from across the fire.

Later, inside, lying in the fine bed in the guest room — linen sheets, a carved headboard, the smell of cedar — he stares at the ceiling for a while. He thinks about the old man walking down the path to the harbor, and the bird that was there when the man was not.

He has been told twice now. He does not need to be told again.

He turns over and sleeps, and he sleeps well, which surprises him.
