Morning. Another sacrifice — a young heifer this time, brought in and killed at the altar while the household gathers. The gold leaf on the horns catches the early light.

Afterwards, Nestor's daughter Polykaste takes Telemakhos inside. She is younger than him, calm and precise in the way of someone raised in a well-ordered house. She takes him to the polished bathtub and pours the water herself — warm, just right. She bathes him and oils him and dresses him in a fresh tunic and cloak from the palace stores. Good cloth, well-woven.

He comes out to where Peisistratos and Nestor are waiting. Nestor looks at him for a moment and something crosses the old man's face — recognition, pleasure, a little grief, all three at once.

"You look like a king's son," Nestor says. He means it as a simple observation. He means it as several other things too.

"Thank you," Telemakhos says. He does not know what else to say. The word is insufficient and he knows it, but it is what he has.

Peisistratos is already checking the horses.
