Nestor speaks for a long time.

He tells what happened after Troy fell. It was not a clean ending. The Greeks were ten years fighting and by the end of it they were not all friends anymore. Agamemnon and Menelaos quarreled — whether to sail immediately or stay and make offerings to appease Athena, who was angry at some of them. The army split. Some went with Agamemnon, some with Menelaos.

Nestor went with Menelaos. They sailed and the gods were mostly fair to them. Nestor came home to Pylos without losing a man. He sat down in his hall and did not move for a long time. He was grateful to be alive.

He tells it plainly, the way old soldiers tell it — without drama, as one thing after another. The places they stopped. The men who died in storms and the men who didn't. The coast of Greece at night, the smell of it, the difference between being out on the water and being on land that was yours.

"I came home," Nestor says. He pauses to let the weight of that settle. "Not everyone did."

Telemakhos is still. He knows what's coming next.
