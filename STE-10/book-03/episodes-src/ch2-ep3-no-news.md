Telemakhos asks the question he has sailed here to ask.

"Do you know anything about my father? Anything at all — where he went after Troy, which way he sailed, anyone who saw him? Is there any news?"

Nestor shakes his head slowly. "Telemakhos. I will tell you honestly. I know nothing about Odysseus after we parted ways. He went with Agamemnon when the fleet split, and after that — I don't know. I came my way and he went his, and I never had word."

He refills his cup. "But I can tell you who might know. Menelaos. He was the last of all the kings to get home — he was blown to Egypt, wandered the edges of the world, came back years after everyone else. He may have passed through places where there was news. Go to Sparta and ask him."

He pauses. "And one more thing. Don't stay away too long. Your mother is alone in that house. Aigisthos spent seven years in Agamemnon's house before he acted. Don't give anyone in Ithaka seven years."

Telemakhos is quiet for a moment. "I understand you," he says.

The day has moved into afternoon. The fires on the beach are low, the black bones scattered, the crowd thinned. The sea is flat and bright in the distance.
