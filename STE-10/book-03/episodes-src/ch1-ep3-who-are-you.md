"Strangers," Nestor says. "Who are you? Where do you come from? Are you merchants? Or do you sail on some other business?"

Telemakhos looks at Mentor. Mentor gives him nothing — just a slight tilt of the head. Go on.

Telemakhos takes a breath and speaks. He tells Nestor who he is: Telemakhos, son of Odysseus, from Ithaka. He says why he has come. His father sailed from Troy and never came home. His estate is being devoured. His mother is besieged. He has come to ask if Nestor has any news of Odysseus — whether he died on the road, or was lost at sea, or is somewhere still alive and trying to get back.

He speaks more steadily than he expected to.

Nestor is quiet for a moment after he finishes. He looks at the young man across the fire. Then he says: "Telemakhos. You remind me of your father when he spoke. You have his directness. He never wasted words either."

He picks up his cup. "I'll tell you everything I know," he says. "It will take some time."

The fire crackles. The sun is climbing. Around them the sacrifice goes on.
