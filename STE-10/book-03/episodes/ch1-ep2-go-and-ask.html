Chapter 1 · Episode 1.2

Go and Ask

Book III, lines 36–101 — Telemakhos is afraid to approach. Mentor pushes. Peisistratos comes to meet them.

They beach the ship and Telemakhos doesn't move.

"I've never done this before," he says to Mentor. He is looking at the vast assembly on the shore. "I don't know how to talk to kings. What do I say? I'm not practiced at making speeches to old men. And Nestor — he's ancient, he fought at Troy, everyone honors him. How do I just walk up and ask if he knows anything about my father?"

Mentor looks at him. "Some of it you'll figure out yourself," he says. "And some of it the gods will give you. Walk down. You didn't sail across the sea to stand in the ship."

So they go down together. And before they have gone ten steps, Nestor's son Peisistratos is walking toward them — a young man, easy in his own skin, with the confident hospitality of someone raised in a great household. He takes their hands and leads them to the feast without asking who they are. He gives them food, gives them the choicest cuts, pours wine. He waits until they have eaten before asking anything.

Telemakhos eats. He watches Nestor from across the fire — a very old man, broad across the shoulders still, with the settled stillness of someone who has seen everything and is no longer surprised by any of it.

Nestor's eyes move to the strangers. He sets down his cup and speaks.
