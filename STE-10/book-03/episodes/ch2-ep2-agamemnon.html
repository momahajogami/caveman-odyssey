Chapter 2 · Episode 2.2

Agamemnon

Book III, lines 193–316 — The murder, the revenge, and what Nestor is really saying to Telemakhos.

Nestor tells him about Agamemnon.

Agamemnon came home after ten years away — the greatest king in Greece, the commander of the whole fleet, the man who took Troy. He came home to his own palace and his own wife and his own table. And Aigisthos — a man who had stayed behind, had not sailed to Troy, had spent ten years in Agamemnon's house getting comfortable — Aigisthos killed him. Killed him at the dinner table. Seduced his wife first. Waited for the right moment and then killed him.

"Aigisthos ruled for seven years," Nestor says. "Seven years, in a dead man's house, with a dead man's wife. And then Orestes came home."

Orestes was Agamemnon's son. He had been away — kept safe while Aigisthos held the throne. When he came back he killed Aigisthos and his mother Klytaimnestra, who had been complicit in the murder. He did what a son does.

Nestor lets the silence sit for a moment. Then he looks directly at Telemakhos. "You see why I tell you this."

Telemakhos meets the old man's eyes. "I see."

"Orestes has fame now," Nestor says. "People speak well of him. He did a hard thing and he did it. Are you made of the same material as Orestes? You look like it. You look like your father, which means you look like the kind of man who acts."

Outside the fire is lower. The sun has moved.
