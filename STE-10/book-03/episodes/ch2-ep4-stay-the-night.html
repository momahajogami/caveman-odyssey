Chapter 2 · Episode 2.4

Stay the Night

Book III, lines 317–403 — Nestor insists. Mentor goes to the ship. Athena flies away as a bird.

When the afternoon light begins to go orange, Nestor stands and says: "You're staying."

It is not a question. Telemakhos starts to say that his companion is on the ship, and that he doesn't want to impose, and Nestor waves all of this away. "Mentor can sleep on the ship if he wants to. You are staying in the palace. I am not letting the son of Odysseus sleep aboard a ship when there are beds in my house."

Mentor — the old man who has been quiet for most of the afternoon — stands and says he will indeed go to the ship. He gives Telemakhos a look that means: you're fine, stay. Then he turns and walks away down toward the harbor.

They all watch him go. He is perhaps fifty steps down the path when a large bird — a sea-eagle, swift and low — comes from nowhere and is suddenly flying where Mentor was walking, and Mentor is no longer there.

Nobody speaks for a moment.

Nestor is the first to recover. He reaches out and takes Telemakhos's arm. His voice is quiet. "The friend who traveled with you — that was not a mortal man." He looks at the sky where the bird has gone. "One of the gods walked with you from Ithaka. Athena. She honors you. She honored your father the same way."

He turns back to the fire. His face is moved, and unashamed of it.

"Come," he says. "Let us make offerings to her tonight."
