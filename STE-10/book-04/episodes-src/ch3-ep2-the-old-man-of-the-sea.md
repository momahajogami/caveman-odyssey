They hid in the sealskins and lay down among the real seals and waited.

The smell, Menelaos says, was extraordinary. Eidothea had put ambrosia under their noses to help, but there is only so much ambrosia can do against a herd of seals on a warm beach. They lay there for hours.

At noon Proteus came up out of the sea. He walked among his seals, counting them — one, two, three, moving along the rows — and he counted Menelaos and his three men too, and did not notice. Then he lay down and went to sleep.

They jumped him. All four, one on each limb. He was old and he was small but what he could do was change. He became a lion — they held on. He became a snake — they held on. He became a leopard, a great boar, running water, a tall tree in full leaf. They held on through everything. They did not let go and they did not talk to each other because there was nothing to say — there was only holding.

Finally Proteus stopped. He lay still in his own shape — the old man again, small and gray and wet from the sea — and he looked up at them.

"All right," he said. "Which god told you to do this? What do you want to know?"

Menelaos told him.
