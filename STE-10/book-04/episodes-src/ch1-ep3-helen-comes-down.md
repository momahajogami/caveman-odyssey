Helen comes down the stairs.

She is attended by servants — one carrying her silver basket, one with a spindle, one with the wool. She moves through the hall and the noise settles around her the way water settles around a stone.

She is still beautiful. It is the first thing everyone notices and the last thing that matters to her. She sits and looks at the two young men Menelaos has brought in.

"Menelaos," she says. "Who are these young men? I have never seen anyone look so much like one person in my life." She is looking at Telemakhos. "This is the son of Odysseus. This is Telemakhos — he was a baby when his father went to Troy. He went to Troy for my sake." She says the last sentence plainly, without drama or excuse. It is simply what she carries. "I have never stopped knowing that."

Menelaos looks at the young man. "Yes. The same hands. The same way of holding his head. I was just speaking of his father."

Peisistratos speaks up: "Yes — this is Telemakhos, son of Odysseus. He comes to ask if you have news of his father. I am Peisistratos, son of Nestor. My own brother died at Troy — Antilochos. I never knew him. I carry that." He says it without self-pity. He is reporting a fact about himself.

Menelaos is quiet for a moment. He looks at both of them. "Antilochos," he says. "I knew him. He was everything they say." He looks at the table. "And now you are here. Both of you are welcome in this house as if you were my own sons. Eat. Rest. Tomorrow we talk about fathers."

He lifts his cup. "Tonight: eat."
