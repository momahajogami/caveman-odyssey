The conversation keeps circling back to the dead.

They are talking about Antilochos again — Menelaos remembering him, Peisistratos listening with the particular attention of someone who has heard a story all his life but never from a witness. And Telemakhos is quiet, which he has been since the cloak-over-the-eyes moment, which everyone is politely not acknowledging.

Helen watches all of this from across the table.

She reaches under her chair and takes out a wooden box. From it she takes a small amount of something — a powder, pale, not much — and she puts it into the wine bowl and mixes it in while everyone is talking.

She refills their cups.

No one notices anything immediately. But within a few minutes the weight in the room is different. Not gone — nothing as crude as gone. Just lighter. The grief is still there but it sits differently, like a stone lifted from water. Menelaos stops mid-sentence and breathes more easily. Peisistratos's shoulders come down. Even Telemakhos looks up.

"It came from Egypt," Helen says, when she sees their faces change. "A gift from Polydamna — a healer there. The Egyptians know more about plants than anyone. This one: a man whose father and mother both died that morning, if he drinks this, will not weep that night." She pauses. "I don't use it often. Tonight seemed right."

She sets the box away. "Now let me tell you a story about Odysseus."
