Chapter 1 · Episode 1.2

The Palace

Book IV, lines 68–167 — Gold everywhere. Silver. Ivory. Menelaos speaks of grief.

The hall stops Telemakhos at the threshold.

Gold on the walls. Silver on the columns. Bronze fittings on the doors. Ivory inlay on the furniture. Amber, electrum, polished stone. More wealth than he has ever seen assembled in one place — more wealth than he knew existed. It catches the torchlight and gives it back multiplied.

He leans to Peisistratos and says, quietly: "This must be what the inside of Zeus's palace looks like."

Menelaos hears him — or sees his face, which says the same thing. He crosses the hall and takes both their hands and seats them and brings food himself. He does it easily, the way he does everything. Then he sits across from them.

"Yes," he says, glancing around. "Much gold. Much everything. I went very far to get it — eight years wandering after Troy before I made it home. Egypt, Phoinikia, Libya, the far edges of the world." He pauses. "And while I was gone, someone killed my brother."

The hall is full of music and talk. Menelaos sits in the middle of it and speaks quietly.

"I have all this gold. I would give all of it — every piece — to have back three men who died. And most of all one man." He looks at the table. "Somewhere his father grieves. Somewhere his wife waits. Somewhere his son grew up without him." He stops. "I can't even speak his name without—"

He stops again.

Across the table, Telemakhos pulls his cloak over his eyes. He turns his face away.

Menelaos sees. He says nothing. He lets the moment be what it is.
