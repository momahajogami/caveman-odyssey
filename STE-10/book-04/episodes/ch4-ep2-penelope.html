Chapter 4 · Episode 4.2

Penelope

Book IV, lines 675–767 — She learns her son is gone. She sits on the floor and weeps.

Penelope hears it from a herald who did not know he should not tell her.

He comes in with some other message, and in the course of delivering it he mentions, in passing, that Telemakhos has sailed. He says it the way you say something you assume the other person already knows.

Penelope sits down on the floor.

The servants come and crowd around her. She cannot speak for a long time. When she does her voice is not her usual voice.

"Why didn't he tell me?" she says. "Why didn't he say anything? Odysseus is already gone — he has been gone twenty years. And now Telemakhos—" She stops. "He is sixteen. He has never sailed anywhere. He has never been anywhere. He is gone to Pylos in a borrowed ship and the suitors are going to kill him and I am going to lose both of them."

The servants are quiet. There is nothing useful to say.

She asks for Eurykleia. Eurykleia comes in and Penelope can see immediately on the old nurse's face that she already knew — that this is the face of someone who has been keeping a secret for as long as they could stand it.

Penelope stares at her. "You knew."

Eurykleia tells her everything. The provisions, the oath, the twelve days. "He made me swear," she says. "He said — don't tell her. He wanted to spare you the worrying." She kneels on the floor beside her. "He is your son. He is his father's son. He will come back."

Penelope sits on the floor and Eurykleia kneels beside her and outside the suitors' voices go on.
