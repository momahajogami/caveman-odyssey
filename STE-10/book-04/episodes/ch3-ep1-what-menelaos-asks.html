Chapter 3 · Episode 3.1

What Menelaos Heard

Book IV, lines 290–425 — Morning. Telemakhos asks directly. Menelaos tells how he found out.

Morning. The wedding guests are gone or sleeping. The palace is quieter than it was.

Telemakhos comes to Menelaos before breakfast and says: "I need to ask you directly. Do you have any news of my father? Any at all?"

Menelaos is quiet for a moment. Then he says: "Sit down. I'll tell you how I know what I know — because where I heard it from matters."

On his way home from Troy, Menelaos was stuck on an island called Pharos, off the coast of Egypt, for twenty days. No wind. No way to leave. Supplies running out, crew getting desperate. And a sea-nymph named Eidothea took pity on him and came to him on the beach.

She told him: my father is the Old Man of the Sea — Proteus, who serves Poseidon. He comes to this island every day at noon to sleep among his seals. He knows everything — every sea, every god, every fate. If you can catch him and hold him, he has to tell you what you want to know. But he will not come quietly. He changes shape. He will become anything — lion, snake, water, fire. You have to hold on no matter what he becomes, until he gives up and answers you.

Eidothea gave them sealskins to wear as disguise and showed them where Proteus slept.

"We held on," Menelaos says.
